Joy can sometimes feel fragile.

A pleasant conversation can brighten our day.

An unexpected phone call can change it.

Good news lifts our spirits.

Disappointment can weigh heavily upon our hearts.

If our joy depends only on what happens around us, it will rise and fall with every circumstance.

Paul knew that better than most.

He wrote these words while imprisoned.

His freedom had been taken away.

His future remained uncertain.

Yet from that prison cell comes one of the most joyful commands in all of Scripture.

Not simply to rejoice.

But to rejoice in the Lord.

That small phrase makes all the difference.

## Scripture Reading

<sup class="verse-num">4</sup> Rejoice in the Lord alway: and again I say, Rejoice.

## Devotional Reading

Paul does *not* ignore suffering.

He had experienced it firsthand.

He knew what it meant to be beaten,

shipwrecked,

misunderstood,

and imprisoned.

Yet he also knew something that hardship could never touch.

Christ had not changed.

The Lord was still faithful.

Still present.

Still reigning.

That's why Paul says,

"Rejoice in the Lord."

Our circumstances may change from one day to the next.

But the Lord does not.

His love remains constant.

His promises remain sure.

His mercy is new every morning.

When our joy is rooted in Him,

it becomes deeper than happiness.

It becomes the quiet confidence that God is good, even when life is difficult.

We often think of joy as an emotion.

Something that comes and goes.

But Scripture speaks of joy as one of the fruits of the Holy Spirit.

That means joy is something God grows within us.

There will be days when we don't feel joyful.

There will be seasons marked by grief,

uncertainty,

or loss.

Even then,

the roots of joy can continue to grow beneath the surface.

Joy is not pretending that everything is fine.

It is trusting that God remains faithful when everything is not.

It's the steady assurance that Christ is with us,

working in ways we cannot yet see,

and leading us toward a hope that will *never* disappoint.

Today, pause for a moment before rushing into the demands of the day.

Instead of beginning with your worries,

begin with gratitude.

Thank God for one specific blessing.

Then another.

Perhaps it's the gift of a new morning.

The love of your family.

The forgiveness you have received through Christ.

The hope of eternal life.

The more we remember who God is,

the more reasons we discover to rejoice.

Joy grows where gratitude is cultivated.

And gratitude flourishes when our eyes remain fixed on the Lord.

## Closing Prayer

*Heavenly Father,*

*Thank You for being the unchanging source of true joy.*

*When my circumstances are pleasant,*

*help me to rejoice in You rather than in passing comforts.*

*When life becomes difficult,*

*help me to remember that Your faithfulness has not changed.*

*Fill my heart with the joy that comes from knowing Christ.*

*Teach me to give thanks in every season,*

*to trust You in every trial,*

*and to praise You even when I cannot yet see the outcome.*

*May the joy of the Lord become my strength,*

*quieting my fears,*

*lifting my spirit,*

*and reminding me each day that I belong to You.*

*For no circumstance can separate me from Your love,*

*and no sorrow can diminish the hope You have given through Your Son.*

*To You be all glory,*

*together with the Son and the Holy Spirit,*

*now and ever and unto ages of ages.*

*Amen.*
