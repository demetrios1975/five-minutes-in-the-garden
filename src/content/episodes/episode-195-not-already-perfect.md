There are few things more discouraging

than feeling like we should be farther along than we are.

Perhaps we thought we would have conquered a particular temptation by now.

Perhaps we imagined our faith would be stronger,

our patience deeper,

or our prayers more consistent.

It’s easy to become frustrated by our own shortcomings.

But the Christian life has never been about reaching perfection through our own efforts.

It is about faithfully following the One who is patiently transforming us.

Even the Apostle Paul,

after years of ministry,

suffering,

and faithful service,

did not claim that he had arrived.

Instead,

he continued pressing forward with humility and hope.

## Scripture Reading

<sup class="verse-num">12</sup> Not as though I had already attained, either were already perfect: but I follow after, if that I may apprehend that for which also I am apprehended of Christ Jesus.

## Devotional Reflection

Paul could have listed countless reasons to be confident.

He had preached the Gospel across nations.

He had planted churches.

He had endured persecution with unwavering faith.

Yet he refused to present himself as someone who had reached the finish line.

Instead,

he confessed that Christ was still shaping him.

There is tremendous comfort in those words.

If Paul understood that he was still growing,

then we should not be surprised that God is still working in us as well.

The Christian life is not a race to become flawless.

It’s a lifelong journey of becoming more like Christ.

Each day,

the Holy Spirit patiently forms us,

corrects us,

strengthens us,

and draws us closer to the image of our Savior.

One phrase in this verse deserves special attention.

Paul says he presses on

“that I may apprehend that for which also I am apprehended of Christ Jesus.”

Before Paul ever reached for Christ,

Christ reached for him.

On the road to Damascus,

Paul was not searching for Jesus.

Jesus came searching for Paul.

The same is true for us.

Our pursuit of Christ begins because He first pursued us.

Our faith rests not in the strength of our grip on Him,

but in the faithfulness of His grip on us.

That truth gives us courage when we stumble.

We continue pressing forward

because the One who called us has not let go.

If you’ve become discouraged by your spiritual progress,

remember that God measures faithfulness differently than the world measures success.

Do not compare your journey with someone else’s.

Do not let yesterday’s failures convince you to stop walking today.

Instead,

thank God that He is still at work.

Every prayer offered.

Every act of repentance.

Every quiet step of obedience.

These are signs of His grace working within you.

*The Lord is not finished with you.*

Keep walking.

Keep trusting.

Keep following the One who first laid hold of your heart.

## Closing Prayer

*Lord Jesus,*

*Thank You for calling me to Yourself and never letting me go.*

*When I become discouraged by my weaknesses,*

*remind me that You are still shaping my life.*

*Give me patience as I grow,*

*humility as I learn,*

*and perseverance when the path seems long.*

*Help me not to measure myself by worldly standards,*

*but by a heart that continues to seek after You.*

*Thank You that my hope does not rest in my own strength,*

*but in Your unfailing faithfulness.*

*Continue the good work You have begun within me.*

*Lead me one step at a time,*

*until the day I stand complete in Your presence.*

*For You are the Author and Finisher of my faith,*

*and I gladly place my life into Your hands.*

*In the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit,*

*Amen.*
