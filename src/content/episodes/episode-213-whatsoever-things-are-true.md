Our minds are constantly being filled with something.

News headlines.

Conversations.

Memories.

Worries.

Plans for tomorrow.

Before long, our thoughts begin to shape our attitudes, our words, and even the way we see the world.

Paul understood this.

After encouraging us to replace anxiety with prayer and reminding us that God’s peace will guard our hearts, he turns to another important part of the Christian life.

He speaks about the discipline of the mind.

Peace *does not* come from emptying our thoughts.

It comes from filling them with what’s true.

Today, Paul gives us a beautiful pattern for cultivating a mind that rests in God.

## Scripture Reading

<sup class="verse-num">8</sup> Finally, brethren, whatsoever things are true, whatsoever things are honest, whatsoever things are just, whatsoever things are pure, whatsoever things are lovely, whatsoever things are of good report; if there be any virtue, and if there be any praise, think on these things.

## Devotional Reading

Paul doesn’t tell us simply to stop thinking about anxious things.

Instead, he gives our minds a new place to dwell.

Think about what is true.

What is honorable.

What is just.

What is pure.

What is lovely.

What is worthy of praise.

Notice that these aren’t merely positive thoughts.

They’re godly thoughts.

Thoughts that reflect the character of God Himself.

Our minds cannot remain empty for long.

If we continually feed them with fear,

resentment,

or endless distractions,

our hearts will eventually follow.

But when we meditate upon the truth of God,

His peace begins to shape us from the inside out.

Imagine walking through a garden every day.

If you continually water the flowers,

they flourish.

If you continually feed the weeds,

they spread.

Our thoughts work in much the same way.

Whatever we repeatedly dwell upon begins to take root.

That’s why Scripture calls us again and again to meditate upon God’s Word.

Not because He wishes to burden us with another task,

but because He knows that His truth brings life.

When our minds return to His promises,

our fears begin to lose their voice.

When our hearts dwell upon His goodness,

gratitude quietly takes the place of complaint.

The thoughts we nurture today often become the character we display tomorrow.

Today, pay attention to what occupies your thoughts.

When worry begins to take root,

answer it with one of God’s promises.

When criticism fills your mind,

look for something worthy of gratitude.

When discouragement whispers that all is lost,

remember the faithfulness of Christ.

Choose one verse of Scripture today and carry it with you.

Return to it throughout the day.

Allow God’s truth to become the steady rhythm of your thinking.

As a gardener patiently tends the soil,

so the Holy Spirit patiently cultivates our hearts through the truth we choose to treasure.

## Closing Prayer

*Heavenly Father,*

*Thank You for caring not only about my actions,*

*but also about my thoughts.*

*You know how easily my mind can become distracted,*

*discouraged,*

*or overwhelmed.*

*Fill my heart with what is true.*

*Teach me to delight in what is pure,*

*to seek what is honorable,*

*and to treasure whatever reflects Your goodness.*

*When anxious thoughts begin to grow,*

*replace them with confidence in Your promises.*

*When fear speaks loudly,*

*help me to hear Your voice more clearly.*

*May Your Word take deep root within me,*

*bearing the fruit of peace,*

*wisdom,*

*and steadfast faith.*

*Guard my mind in Christ Jesus,*

*and let every thought become another opportunity to know and love You more deeply.*

*For You are the source of all truth,*

*all beauty,*

*and all goodness.*

*In the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit,*

*Amen.*
