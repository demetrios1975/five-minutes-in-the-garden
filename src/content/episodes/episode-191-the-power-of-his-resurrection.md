When we hear the word “resurrection,”

our thoughts naturally turn to Easter morning.

The empty tomb.

The stone rolled away.

The joyful proclamation that Christ is risen.

Those events stand at the very heart of our faith.

But Paul reminds us

that the resurrection is more than something that happened long ago.

It is a living reality.

The same power that raised Christ from the dead

continues to work in the lives of His people.

It gives hope where there was despair.

Strength where there was weakness.

And new life where sin once held us captive.

That is the power Paul longed to know more deeply.

And it is the same power available to every believer today.

## Scripture Reading

<sup class="verse-num">10</sup> That I may know Him, and the power of His resurrection, and the fellowship of His sufferings, being made conformable unto His death.

## Devotional Reflection

When Paul speaks about “the power of His resurrection,”

he is not speaking only about life after death.

He is speaking about life today.

The risen Christ is actively at work in His people.

He raises discouraged hearts.

He restores broken lives.

He strengthens those who are weary.

He frees us from the chains of sin.

Resurrection power is God’s life overcoming everything that leads toward death.

Every act of forgiveness.

Every victory over temptation.

Every step of faith taken in difficult circumstances

is evidence that Christ is alive and working within us.

His resurrection is not merely history.

It is our hope.

Sometimes we imagine spiritual growth as one dramatic moment.

But more often,

resurrection unfolds quietly.

A heart that slowly becomes more forgiving.

A habit that gradually loses its grip.

A person who begins to trust God after years of fear.

These are not small things.

They are signs of new life.

The Lord rarely changes us all at once.

Instead,

He patiently transforms us day by day.

Just as spring slowly awakens a garden after a long winter,

the risen Christ patiently brings new life to every part of our hearts.

His work is steady.

Gentle.

And faithful.

Today,

do not define yourself by yesterday’s failures.

Do not assume that old habits or old fears

must always have the final word.

You belong to the risen Christ.

His grace is still at work.

His Spirit is still transforming you.

Where you need forgiveness,

receive it.

Where you need strength,

ask for it.

Where you need hope,

look again to the empty tomb.

Because the power that raised Jesus from the dead

is still bringing new life to His people.

Walk today as someone who has been made new.

## Closing Prayer

*Risen Lord,*

*I praise You because You are alive.*

*Death could not hold You.*

*The grave could not keep You.*

*And because You live,*

*hope is never lost.*

*Breathe Your resurrection life into every part of my heart.*

*Where I have grown weary,*

*renew me.*

*Where fear has taken root,*

*fill me with courage.*

*Where old habits still cling to me,*

*grant me the strength to walk in freedom.*

*Thank You that Your work in me is not finished.*

*Each new day is another reminder that Your mercy is fresh,*

*Your grace is sufficient,*

*and Your life is stronger than my weakness.*

*May I live today as one who belongs to the risen Christ,*

*bearing witness to the hope that can only be found in You.*

*For You have conquered sin,*

*You have conquered death,*

*and You reign forever with the Father and the Holy Spirit,*

*now and ever and unto ages of ages.*

*Amen.*
