There are some truths

that are difficult to speak.

Not because they are unclear.

But because they break our hearts.

A loving parent

doesn’t enjoy warning a child about danger.

A faithful physician

doesn’t delight in delivering a difficult diagnosis.

Neither did the Apostle Paul

take pleasure in speaking

about those who had turned away from Christ.

His words today aren’t harsh.

They are sorrowful.

He speaks,

not with anger,

but with tears.

In doing so,

he reminds us

that truth and compassion

are never meant to be enemies.

The Christian heart

holds them together.

## Scripture Reading

<sup class="verse-num">18</sup> (For many walk, of whom I have told you often, and now tell you even weeping, that they are the enemies of the cross of Christ:)

## Devotional Reflection

Paul had already warned the Philippians

about false teachers

and those who opposed the Gospel.

Yet he returns to the subject once more.

Why?

Because eternal things matter.

Notice, however,

how he speaks.

“I now tell you even weeping.”

His concern is not driven by self-righteousness.

He is not trying to win an argument.

He grieves over those

who have rejected the way of Christ.

That’s the heart of a shepherd.

It’s also the heart of Christ Himself.

Jesus wept over Jerusalem

because the people refused the peace He offered.

He looked upon the crowds with compassion

because they were like sheep without a shepherd.

Truth without love becomes cold.

Love without truth becomes empty.

In Christ,

we find both perfectly united.

It is easy to read a verse like this

and immediately think of someone else.

Someone whose life has wandered.

Someone whose beliefs differ from our own.

But Scripture first invites us

to examine ourselves.

Are there places

where I resist the way of the cross?

The cross calls us to humility.

To forgiveness.

To self-denial.

To sacrificial love.

Whenever we choose pride instead of humility…

resentment instead of forgiveness…

or comfort instead of obedience…

we are moving away

from the pattern Christ has set before us.

The call of this passage

is not merely to recognize error in others.

It is to cling more closely to Christ ourselves.

Today,

pray for someone

who seems far from God.

Do not pray with frustration.

Pray with compassion.

Ask the Lord

to soften their heart.

Ask Him

to open doors for the Gospel.

Ask Him

to help you become a faithful witness—

not only through your words,

but through your life.

And if the Lord gives you an opportunity

to speak the truth,

do so with the same spirit

Paul displayed.

Not to condemn.

Not to prove yourself right.

But with genuine love

for the person standing before you.

The cross teaches us

that God’s truth

is always carried

by God’s love.

## Closing Prayer

*Merciful Father,*

*Your heart is full of compassion for those who have wandered from You.*

*Teach me to see others through Your eyes.*

*Guard me from becoming proud when I speak the truth,*

*or silent when love requires me to speak.*

*Fill my heart with the same tenderness that moved Paul to tears,*

*and the same compassion that led Christ to the cross.*

*Strengthen me to remain faithful when the world follows a different path.*

*Help me to walk humbly,*

*to love generously,*

*and to bear witness to the hope of the Gospel with both conviction and grace.*

*And whenever I am tempted to drift from the way of the cross,*

*draw me back to Your side.*

*For there is no safer place than following the footsteps of Your Son,*

*whose love conquered sin,*

*whose cross brought salvation,*

*and whose resurrection gives eternal life.*

*In the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit,*

*Amen.*
