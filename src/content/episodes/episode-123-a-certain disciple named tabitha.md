The book of Acts is filled with dramatic moments.

Miracles.

Preaching.

Journeys.

Transformation.

But today, we pause to notice someone
whose faithfulness looked quieter.

A woman named Tabitha.

She is not introduced through power
or public influence.

Instead, Scripture remembers her
for something deeply simple:

Her goodness.

Her generosity.

Her care for others.

And that matters more
than we sometimes realize.

## Scripture Reading

<sup class="verse-num">36</sup> Now there was at Joppa a certain disciple named Tabitha, which by interpretation is called Dorcas: this woman was full of good works and almsdeeds which she did.

## Devotional Reflection

Tabitha’s life reminds us
that spiritual significance isn’t always loud.

Sometimes holiness looks like kindness.

Patience.

Generosity.

Steady acts of care.

Scripture doesn’t say
she occasionally did good works.

It says she was full of them.

Compassion had become part of who she was.

Many people wonder
whether their lives are making a difference.

They compare themselves to louder voices,

larger platforms,

more visible callings.

But Tabitha reminds us
that love lived consistently
leaves a deep mark on the world.

Not every meaningful life
draws attention.

Some simply bless everyone nearby.

Today, don’t underestimate
small acts of goodness.

A kind word.

A thoughtful gesture.

A patient response.

A willingness to help quietly.

These things matter.

You may never fully see
the impact they have on others.

But God sees.

And over time,
a life shaped by compassion
becomes a testimony all its own.

## Closing Prayer

*Lord,*

*Thank You for reminding me*
*that faithfulness is not measured*
*only through visible accomplishments.*

*You value kindness,*
*compassion,*
*and quiet acts of love.*

*Help me not to overlook*
*the small opportunities to care for others each day.*

*Give me a heart that is attentive to need,*
*willing to serve,*
*and generous without seeking recognition.*

*Teach me to live faithfully*
*in ordinary moments—*
*to speak gently,*
*to give freely,*
*and to love consistently.*

*And when I wonder*
*whether my efforts matter,*
*remind me that no act of love*
*is unseen by You.*

*Shape my life into something*
*that quietly reflects Your goodness*
*to those around me.*

*In the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit,*
*Amen.*
