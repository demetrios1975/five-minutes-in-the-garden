Every journey

has a destination.

When we begin a trip,

we usually know where we’re headed

before we ever turn the key in the ignition.

That destination

determines every road we choose

along the way.

The same is true spiritually.

Our daily decisions

are *not* isolated moments.

They become the direction of our lives.

Little by little,

choice by choice,

we’re being shaped

into the people we are becoming.

Paul continues the sober warning

he began in yesterday’s passage.

But once again,

his purpose isn’t to frighten his readers.

*It’s to awaken them.*

Because God desires

that every person

would turn toward the life

He offers in Christ.

## Scripture Reading

<sup class="verse-num">19</sup> Whose end is destruction, whose God is their belly, and whose glory is in their shame, who mind earthly things.

## Devotional Reflection

Paul describes those

who have rejected the way of the cross

by speaking about the direction of their hearts.

Their desires

have become their master.

Their values

have become distorted.

Their attention

is fixed only upon what is temporary.

This is more than a description

of outward behavior.

It’s a description

of misplaced worship.

Every human heart

is created to worship.

If we don’t worship God,

we’ll inevitably give our devotion

to something else.

Comfort.

Success.

Pleasure.

Power.

Approval.

None of these things

are evil in themselves.

But when they become

the center of our lives,

they begin to shape us

in ways they were never meant to.

Christ alone

is worthy of occupying that place.

Paul ends the verse

by saying they “mind earthly things.”

That phrase

invites each of us

to pause and reflect.

What occupies my thoughts most often?

What consumes my energy?

What do I pursue

when no one else is watching?

There’s nothing wrong

with caring for our families,

working diligently,

or enjoying God’s good gifts.

The danger comes

when the temporary

crowds out the eternal.

When earthly concerns

become our only concerns.

Jesus reminds us,

“Seek ye first the kingdom of God.”

Not because the things of this world

are unimportant,

but because they find their proper place

only when Christ is first.

When our hearts belong to Him,

we’re free to enjoy His gifts

without becoming enslaved by them.

Take a few moments today

to examine the direction of your heart.

Ask yourself:

What’s occupied my mind this week?

Have my thoughts

drawn me closer to Christ,

or simply deeper

into the distractions of this world?

Then choose one simple way

to lift your eyes toward eternity.

Spend a few extra moments in prayer.

Read a Psalm slowly.

Encourage someone

who needs hope.

Offer thanks to God

before rushing into the next task.

Small acts of faithfulness

have a way of gently reorienting our hearts.

And when our hearts

are fixed upon Christ,

our lives begin to reflect His peace.

## Closing Prayer

*Heavenly Father,*

*You alone are worthy of my worship.*

*Forgive me for the times I become distracted by things that cannot satisfy the deepest longings of my heart.*

*When the cares of this world begin to crowd out my love for You,*

*gently draw my attention back to Your presence.*

*Teach me to hold earthly blessings with gratitude,*

*but never more tightly than I hold You.*

*Shape my desires so that they reflect the heart of Christ.*

*May my thoughts be filled with what is true,*

*my words with what is gracious,*

*and my life with what brings glory to Your name.*

*Keep my eyes fixed upon the eternal Kingdom,*

*where Christ reigns forever,*

*and let that hope guide every choice I make today.*

*For You are my portion,*

*my treasure,*

*and my everlasting joy.*

*In the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit,*

*Amen.*
