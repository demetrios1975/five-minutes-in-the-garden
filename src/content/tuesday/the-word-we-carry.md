## Tuesday Transformation

# The Word We Carry

> “Preach the word; be instant in season, out of season; reprove, rebuke, exhort with all longsuffering and doctrine.”
>
> — **2 Timothy 4:2**

What do we carry with us?

Our phones.

Our keys.

Our schedules.

The responsibilities waiting for us when the day begins.

And there are things we carry that no one else can see.

Worries.

Memories.

Ambitions.

Disappointments.

Questions about tomorrow.

Today the Church commemorates the **Holy Martyr and Archdeacon Euplus of Catania**, a man remembered for carrying something else.

He carried the Gospel.

Not merely as a book in his hands.

He carried the Word of God into the way he lived, the way he spoke, and ultimately, the way he faced death.

## The Gospel in His Hands

St. Euplus lived in Sicily at the beginning of the fourth century, during one of the most dangerous periods to openly profess the Christian faith.

Under the persecution of the emperors Diocletian and Maximian, Christians could face imprisonment, torture, and death for refusing to renounce Christ.

Yet Euplus continued to proclaim the Gospel.

Tradition remembers him carrying the sacred writings with him and openly teaching from them. He was eventually arrested while reading and explaining the Gospel to others.

The authorities could imprison the man.

But they could not silence the Word he carried.

Euplus was questioned and commanded to surrender his faith.

He refused.

The Gospel in his hands had already become the conviction of his heart.

## The Gospel in His Heart

There’s a difference between possessing the Scriptures and allowing the Scriptures to possess us.

A Bible can sit on a shelf.

Its words can be memorized.

Its passages can be discussed and debated.

But the Word of God is meant to become something more.

It’s meant to shape us.

Euplus’s witness reveals what that can look like.

After his arrest, he endured imprisonment and suffering. Yet when he was brought again before the authorities, he continued to confess Christ.

Even when he was led toward martyrdom, the Gospel remained associated with him. Tradition tells us that it was hung around his neck as he was taken to execution.

What his persecutors intended as humiliation became an extraordinary testimony.

The condemned man was still carrying the Gospel.

And when Euplus was permitted to speak, he did what he had been doing before his arrest.

He proclaimed Christ.

The circumstances had changed.

His faith had not.

## In Season and Out of Season

St. Paul’s words to Timothy take on particular force when considered beside the life of Euplus:

> “Preach the word; be instant in season, out of season.”

There are seasons when faithfulness feels easy.

When prayer comes naturally.

When belief is encouraged.

When speaking about Christ costs very little.

And then there are other seasons.

Seasons when obedience becomes inconvenient.

When forgiveness is difficult.

When following Christ places us against the expectations of the world around us.

Euplus did not possess one faith for favorable circumstances and another for difficult ones.

He carried the same Gospel into both.

That’s part of what Christian faithfulness asks of us.

Not necessarily that we’ll face the suffering Euplus endured, but that the Christ we confess in peaceful moments will remain the Christ we follow when faithfulness becomes costly.

## The Gospel in Our Lives

Most likely, we’ll never stand before a Roman governor and be ordered to renounce Christ.

But every day presents smaller opportunities to decide what we truly carry within us.

We carry the Gospel when we forgive someone we would rather resent.

We carry it when we speak truth without cruelty.

We carry it when we refuse to participate in something we know is wrong.

We carry it when we show compassion to someone the world has overlooked.

We carry it when we pray instead of surrendering to despair.

And sometimes we carry it simply by remaining faithful when no one else notices.

The Gospel does not become visible in our lives merely because we can quote it.

It becomes visible when its words begin shaping the people we are becoming.

## This Week’s Invitation

Take a few moments to reflect:

- What am I carrying into the world each day?
- Does the way I live reflect the Gospel I profess?
- Where might Christ be asking me to remain faithful even when faithfulness is inconvenient?

Perhaps there’s also a simpler question worth asking.

**If someone encountered my life before they ever opened a Bible, what would my life tell them about Christ?**

## A Closing Reflection

The Church remembers St. Euplus not because he possessed a copy of the Gospel.

Many people possessed books.

We remember him because the Gospel became inseparable from his life.

He carried it when he preached.

He carried it when he was arrested.

He carried it through imprisonment.

And he carried it as he went to his martyrdom.

Eventually, the distinction between the Gospel Euplus held and the Gospel Euplus lived became difficult to separate.

That is the transformation toward which every Christian life should move.

We read the Word so that it may enter our hearts.

We receive it into our hearts so that it may shape our lives.

And we live it so that through us, someone else might encounter Christ.

Euplus carried the Gospel until, in a sense, **the Gospel carried him** through suffering, imprisonment, and even death.

## This Week’s Challenge

Choose one verse of Scripture this week and carry it with you.

Write it down.

Place it somewhere you will see it.

Read it in the morning.

Return to it throughout the day.

But don’t stop with memorizing the words.

Ask God to show you how to **live them**.

Because the Christian calling isn’t simply to own the Word of God.

It’s to allow the Word of God to shape the life we carry into the world.
